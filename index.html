Requirements

What your practice needs

Most private practices already have the essentials.

A

Patient exam space

Your existing exam rooms work. Schedule research visits around normal patient flow.

B

Coordinator workspace

Desk, PC, and reliable internet for your study coordinator. Used for data entry, eCRF, and electronic source documents.

C

Conference / monitor room

Room for sponsor monitor visits, site initiation visits, and training. Can double as a break room or office.

D

Secure drug storage

Lockable room and cabinet for investigational product. Secure refrigerator, freezer, and ambient storage with temperature monitoring.

E

Lab / sample processing

Centrifuge and basic lab supplies. Secure refrigerator and freezer for samples before shipment to the central lab.

F

Additional requirements

Phone/fax line, secure filing for regulatory binders, patient parking, and an after-hours patient contact process.

FAQ

What physicians want to know

Will clinical research disrupt my patient care?

No. Studies fit around your practice flow. Research visits use existing exam rooms during open time slots. Your practice operations stay the priority.

How long before I start earning revenue?

Site setup takes 4–8 weeks. Revenue starts with your first enrolled patient. NeoSci pays within 15–30 days of receiving sponsor payment.

How much of my time does the PI role take?

About 15–30 minutes per patient visit for oversight, plus protocol-specific assessments. NeoSci and your trained coordinator handle the administrative work.

What happens if a sponsor cancels a study mid-trial?

Sponsors pay for all work completed through the cancellation date. You get paid for every visit and procedure already performed.

What if a study isn't a good fit for my practice?

You approve every study. NeoSci matches opportunities to your specialty, patient demographics, and capacity. You never have to accept a study.

Do I need prior research experience?

No. NeoSci provides GCP training, protocol-specific training, and ongoing mentorship. Many successful PIs started with no research background.
